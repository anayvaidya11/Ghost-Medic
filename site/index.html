Prototype

A first-aid assistant that works with no signal.

A language model on the phone you already carry turns sensor readings, and what you say, into spoken first-aid steps. No network.

Where the project stands

  • Working

    Offline, end to end, on one laptop: sensor readings change the model's answer. Verified 22 Jul 2026, receipts on Proof.

  • Working

    The wrist unit's code compiles for the real chip, and its maths matches the numbers the sensor's maker publishes. 48 committed checks in all.

  • Stands in

    A laptop stands in for the device you would carry, a cable for the wireless link. Labelled wherever they appear.

  • Simulated

    Every sensor reading here is generated, not measured. Speech-to-text and wound-photo analysis are placeholders that do nothing yet.

The product, as a concept

What it would look like on you

    Wrist sensor accessory. The board and its code exist: hand-routed, compiled, maths tested. No screen, because the board has none.
  • 2
    The cable. Stands in for the wireless link. The software carrying the readings is real.
  • 3
    The device you already carry. The assistant runs on it: numbered steps, spoken, no internet. The software runs today; the phone body is a mockup.

A concept in code, not CAD. The mannequin is an open-source base mesh (MakeHuman, CC0), not a scan of anyone. The one piece of designed hardware is the board on the Hardware page. Drag to turn, scroll to zoom.

The whole system is held together by one line of text

Ten times a second the wrist unit prints one line: the only contract between the parts. Here is one, from a fall:

{"t_ms":300,
 "max30102":{"ok":true,"red":10249,"ir":10398},
 "bmp280":{"ok":true,"temp_c":21.0,"press_pa":101300.2,"alt_m":2.1},
 "lis3dh":{"ok":true,"x_g":1.80,"y_g":1.50,"z_g":1.20,"mag_g":2.62},
 "fall_detected":true}

In plain English

Light sensor working, two readings from it. Pressure sensor working: 21 °C, and 2.1 m higher than where it started. The motion sensor just felt 2.62 g, over two and a half times the pull of gravity. That looked like a fall.

Each sensor reports its own "ok" first, so a broken one says so instead of quietly reading zero. The light readings stay exactly as they came off the sensor: no heart rate is worked out anywhere, on purpose.
