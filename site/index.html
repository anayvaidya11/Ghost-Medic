Prototype

A first-aid assistant that works with no signal.

A wrist unit reads your motion and the air around you. A language model on hardware you carry turns that, and what you say, into spoken first-aid steps. No network.

Where the project stands

  • Working

    The software chain runs end to end, offline, on one laptop: sensor readings change the model's answer. Verified 22 Jul 2026 against a local model, receipts on Proof.

  • Working

    The code for the wrist unit compiles for the real chip, and its maths matches the numbers the sensor's own maker publishes. Nine checks there, and 39 more on the software that decides what the model gets told.

  • Stands in

    A laptop stands in for the pack computer, a cable for the wireless link. Labelled wherever they appear.

  • Simulated

    Every sensor reading here is generated, not measured. Speech-to-text and wound-photo analysis are placeholders that do nothing yet.

Every claim points at a file in the repository.

The product, as a concept

What it would look like on you

  • 1
    Wrist case. Holds the sensor board and its code. Both exist: every connection on the board drawn by hand, the code compiled and its maths tested.
  • 2
    Display. Concept only: the board that was designed has no screen. Today a phone or laptop shows the readings and speaks the steps.
  • 3
    The cable. The software carrying the readings is real and passes every one through unchanged. The cable itself stands in for the wireless link that would replace it.
  • 4
    Belt pack. The computer and the battery in one curved case, sized around the sort of small computer module this would need. Its makers rate that class at 7 to 15 watts. No module has been chosen and nothing has been measured. The software that runs without internet is real; a laptop stands in for the pack today.

A concept, built in code rather than CAD. The body is a placeholder shape too, not a scan of anyone. The only hardware that exists as a design is the circuit board, drawn by hand in KiCad, on the Hardware page. Drag to turn it, scroll to zoom.

The whole system is held together by one line of text

Ten times a second the wrist unit prints one line describing everything it senses. It is the only contract between the parts. Here is one, from a fall:

{"t_ms":300,
 "max30102":{"ok":true,"red":10249,"ir":10398},
 "bmp280":{"ok":true,"temp_c":21.0,"press_pa":101300.2,"alt_m":2.1},
 "lis3dh":{"ok":true,"x_g":1.80,"y_g":1.50,"z_g":1.20,"mag_g":2.62},
 "fall_detected":true}

In plain English

Light sensor working, two readings from it. Pressure sensor working: 21 °C, and 2.1 m higher than where it started. The motion sensor just felt 2.62 g, over two and a half times the pull of gravity. That looked like a fall.

Each sensor reports its own "ok" first, so a broken one says so instead of quietly reading zero. The light readings stay exactly as they came off the sensor: no heart rate is worked out anywhere, on purpose.
